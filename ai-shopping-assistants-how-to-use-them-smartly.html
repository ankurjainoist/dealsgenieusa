🤖 AI Shopping Assistants: How to Use Them Smartly

AI shopping assistants are now built into many shopping apps, and they can save real time when you know how to use them. Amazon's own assistant used to be called Rufus. In May 2026 Amazon renamed it Alexa for Shopping, and it lives inside the Amazon Shopping app and website. Other general AI chatbots can also help you compare products. Here is a practical way to use any of them.

What AI assistants are good at

They are good at turning a vague need into a short list. For example, you can describe who the gift is for, what they like and how you plan to use the item, and ask for a few options to compare. They can also explain the difference between two product types, list questions you should ask before buying, and summarize what a product page says.

Ask better questions

The more specific your question, the better the answer. Mention the use case (a small apartment, travel, a child's age), the features that matter, and anything you want to avoid. Then ask for the trade-offs, not only the winner, so you can see why an option fits.

Double-check the important facts

An AI answer can be wrong or out of date. Before you buy, open the product page and confirm the current price, the delivery date, the size or compatibility, and the return policy. Read a few recent reviews yourself, especially the middle-star ones.

Remember what the assistant knows about you

Some assistants use your shopping activity to personalize suggestions. That can be handy for reordering, but it also means suggestions may lean toward what you have bought before. If you want fresh ideas, say so in your question.

Do not share sensitive details

Never type card numbers, passwords or other private information into a chatbot. Shopping questions do not need them.

A simple routine

Describe the need, ask for two or three options, ask for the trade-offs, then verify the details on the product page before you buy. It takes a few minutes and often saves you from a wrong purchase.
