Halloween falls on October 31, 2026, and the last two weeks are when plans usually come together in a rush. A little structure helps you spend less and still end up with a costume, a decorated entrance and enough treats. This is the checklist we would follow.

Start with the delivery date, not the product

Before you add anything to your cart, look at the delivery estimate on the product page. A great item that arrives on November 2 is no use. For anything you need by Halloween night, aim to order at least a week ahead and be flexible about which version of an item you pick.

Build a costume from pieces

A full packaged costume is convenient, but a base outfit you already own plus one or two accessories (a hat, a mask, face paint, a cape) often looks better and costs less. Pick the one accessory that makes the costume recognizable and keep the rest simple. If the costume is for a child, check the size chart and the material, and make sure it is comfortable enough to wear for a few hours.

Decorate with a few big moments

Instead of scattering lots of tiny items, choose one or two focal points: a lit doorway, a string of lights along the porch, or a single large decoration. Lighting does most of the work in the dark, and battery-powered lights avoid the cable problem if you do not have an outlet nearby.

Treats: buy early and plan the quantity

Candy is the item most likely to sell out close to the day. Estimate how many trick-or-treaters you usually get, add a small buffer, and buy a few days ahead. Keep a separate bowl for allergy-friendly options so every child can pick something.

Safety basics

Use reflective tape or glow sticks on costumes, avoid masks that block vision, and test any lights before the evening. If you are hosting a party, keep walkways clear and pets away from the door.

A simple order of operations

First pick the costume idea and order the key accessory. Next order lighting and one main decoration. Then buy treats a few days ahead. Two days before Halloween, do a final check: does everything work, and does everyone have what they need?
